About

From optical signals to meaningful medical insight.

I am a Ph.D. student in Electrical and Computer Engineering at Johns Hopkins University, where I work with Professor Jin U. Kang in the Intelligent Optical Imaging and Vision Laboratory (iOIV / Kang Lab). I began my doctoral studies in August 2025, following an M.S. in Electrical and Computer Engineering at Johns Hopkins.

My research brings together biomedical optical imaging, computer vision, and deep learning. I am particularly interested in optical coherence tomography (OCT): using it to characterize tissue, reconstruct useful information from light, and support image-guided surgical robotics. My current work spans corneal imaging, computational models of light transport, and AI-assisted medical image analysis.

My undergraduate training at the University of Cincinnati and Chongqing University provided a foundation in mechanical engineering that continues to inform how I approach imaging systems and robotics.

Research

Selected directions & ongoing work

Better images.
More informed interventions.

Connecting high-resolution biomedical imaging with computational methods, from the physics of light to learning-based analysis.

I

Image-guided surgical robotics

OCT-guided robotic microsurgery

Investigating automatic corneal boundary tracking for deep anterior lamellar keratoplasty (DALK). This work combines Kalman filtering and deep learning to interpret OCT data for image-guided intervention.

  • OCT
  • Boundary tracking
  • Kalman filtering
  • Deep learning
Technical focus +

Temporal tracking of corneal interfaces and the integration of model-based estimation with learned image features for robotic microsurgical guidance.

II

Computational optical imaging

Understanding tissue through light

Studying corneal OCT through scattering and attenuation models, Monte Carlo light transport simulation (MCML), and synthetic data generation for learning-based inverse optical property estimation.

  • Corneal OCT
  • Light transport
  • Inverse problems
Technical focus +

Connecting forward simulations of light propagation with inverse models that estimate tissue optical properties from imaging data.

III

AI-assisted medical image analysis

From images to quantitative insight

Exploring U-Net-based segmentation and quantitative analysis of sweat gland and sweat droplet images, alongside an interest in high-resolution 3D/4D OCT and computational biomedical imaging.

  • Medical segmentation
  • U-Net
  • Quantitative imaging
Technical focus +

Using learning-based segmentation to delineate biological structures and support quantitative image analysis.
